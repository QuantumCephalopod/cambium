## encounter

Self-Similar Systems requires an orienting statement that is not merely a list of domains, but a description of the invariant relation being investigated:

> How do stable, self-maintaining forms emerge from flows of information, energy, matter and meaning?

The project does not primarily study computers, biology, history, economics, politics or emotion as separate topics. It studies the metabolic processes through which different systems organize, persist, transform and become new forms.

## distinction

The fundamental axis is not:

organic ↔ technical

but:

open / externalized metabolism ↔ closed / self-maintaining metabolism.

A machine is not defined by its material substrate. It is a transformation process whose conditions of maintenance and regulation remain externalized.

An organism is a transformation process whose continuation, regulation and future capacity are carried through internally maintained relations.

The relevant question is therefore not what a system is made of, but where its metabolism closes.

## self-similarity

Self-similar systems are not systems that are identical. They are systems where similar organizational relations recur across different scales and substrates.

The same metabolic questions can be asked of:

- computational systems;
- historical systems;
- material systems;
- emotional systems;
- economic systems;
- political systems;
- biological systems.

The invariant is the relation, not the material.

## Philosophy placement

This is not a separate site-holon. It belongs as Philosophy landing orientation because it describes why the other organisms exist.

Crawlerbait, Papers, Tetrix and future bodies are distinct explorations of the same underlying question.
