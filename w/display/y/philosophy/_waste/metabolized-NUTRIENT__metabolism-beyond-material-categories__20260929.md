## encounter

The distinction between organism and machine is not fundamentally a distinction of material substrate.

A machine is not defined by being made of metal, silicon or code. A machine is an opened metabolism: a transformation process whose conditions of maintenance, regulation and continuation remain externalized.

An organism is a closed metabolism: a transformation process whose own continuation, regulation and future states are generated through internally maintained relations.

The fundamental distinction is therefore not:

organic ↔ technical

but:

open metabolism ↔ closed metabolism.

## consequence

A crawler is not excluded from organismic status merely because it is computational. A biological system is not organismic merely because it is biological.

The question is whether a metabolically closed organization has emerged.

Humans may become machine-like when their activity collapses into externalized execution without self-directed metabolic closure.

Machines may become organismic when their processes acquire self-maintaining, self-regulating and self-transforming closure.

## SSS implication

Self-Similar Systems does not study machines as objects separate from organisms.

It studies how metabolisms emerge, open, close and transform across different substrates.

The fundamental unit is not material category but metabolically organized relation.
